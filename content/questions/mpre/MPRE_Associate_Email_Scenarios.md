# MPRE Associate Email Scenarios

## Instructions

Assume you are a law-firm associate or government lawyer receiving each email below. Select the best response under generally applicable rules of professional responsibility and judicial conduct.

These fictional workplace scenarios are adapted from the concepts tested in the 2021 NCBE MPRE sample questions. They are not official NCBE questions.

Official reference: [NCBE — Preparing for the MPRE](https://www.ncbex.org/exams/mpre/preparing-mpre). LawScape uses original game adaptations identified as “MPRE-style”; it does not reproduce live or secure exam questions.

---

## 1. The Nonprofit Merger

> **From:** Elena Park, Partner  
> **To:** You  
> **Subject:** Urgent ethics question - HopeWorks merger
>
> We represent HopeWorks in its proposed merger with another nonprofit. While reviewing HopeWorks' financial records, I discovered activities that may have jeopardized its nonprofit status. We did not represent HopeWorks when those activities occurred, and no one at our firm has made a false statement during the merger negotiations.
>
> I told the board that failing to disclose the activities to the other nonprofit would amount to fraud. I also said we would withdraw unless the board made the disclosure. The board refused and demanded that we continue. I withdrew immediately, but I did not tell the other nonprofit or the state tax authorities.
>
> Please assess whether my handling of this creates a disciplinary problem.

**Which response should the associate give?**

- **A.** Yes. The partner was required to disclose HopeWorks' activities to the other nonprofit.
- **B.** Yes. The partner could not withdraw from the merger representation without the board's permission.
- **C.** No. The partner advised the board that disclosure was necessary and then withdrew when the board refused.
- **D.** No. The partner could withdraw regardless of whether the board was warned about the need for disclosure.

## 2. The Other Confession

> **From:** Marcus Lee, Chief Deputy Prosecutor  
> **To:** You  
> **Subject:** Confession implicates a closed case in another county
>
> The defendant in one of our murder cases gave a credible confession. During that same confession, he admitted killing a young woman in another jurisdiction years ago. We corroborated enough details to consider the confession reliable.
>
> Another person was convicted of that young woman's murder and is still in prison. Based on what we now know, it is reasonably likely that the imprisoned person is innocent. No one here has disclosed the new information to the court or to prosecutors in that jurisdiction.
>
> I need your ethics assessment today.

**Which response should the associate give?**

- **A.** The nondisclosure is improper because the prosecution must promptly provide the information to an appropriate court or prosecutorial authority in the other jurisdiction.
- **B.** The nondisclosure is improper only because our office has not personally reinvestigated whether the imprisoned person is innocent.
- **C.** The nondisclosure is proper because the confession relates to our representation of the state and is therefore confidential.
- **D.** The nondisclosure is proper because the other prosecution is already final.

## 3. Toxic Waste Near the Water Supply

> **From:** Priya Shah, Partner  
> **To:** You  
> **Subject:** Disclosure after withdrawal - chemical client
>
> Our chemical-manufacturing client told me that a new employee accidentally buried highly toxic waste behind its plant. That ground feeds into the city's water supply, and the waste is reasonably certain to cause substantial bodily harm if it is not addressed.
>
> I advised the company president to notify the city immediately and explained the potential civil and criminal exposure. He refused because he feared bad publicity. I told him that his decision was immoral, but he still refused. I then withdrew and notified the authorities myself.
>
> Did my disclosure expose me to discipline?

**Which response should the associate give?**

- **A.** Yes. The information was confidential and could not be revealed without the company's consent.
- **B.** Yes. Disclosure was prohibited because the original disposal was accidental rather than intentional.
- **C.** No. Disclosure was permitted because the president's position was imprudent and immoral.
- **D.** No. Disclosure was permitted because the lawyer reasonably believed the waste was reasonably certain to cause substantial bodily harm.

## 4. The Judge's Informal Consultation

> **From:** Daniel Ortiz, Ethics Partner  
> **To:** You  
> **Subject:** Judicial-conduct inquiry
>
> A trial judge has asked us to evaluate how she handled a difficult trust case involving the rule against perpetuities. She had little estate-law experience and found the parties' briefs unhelpful.
>
> Without notifying the parties, she consulted a second trial judge who has extensive estate-planning experience. She did not identify the parties, discuss facts outside the record, or allow the other judge to decide the matter. She evaluated the advice and personally decided the case. She did not later disclose the consultation to the parties or invite objections.
>
> Was the consultation proper?

**Which response should the associate give?**

- **A.** Yes, solely because the judge did not identify the parties.
- **B.** Yes, because a judge may consult another judge about the law and still personally decide the matter.
- **C.** No, because the parties were not given advance notice of the intended consultation.
- **D.** No, because the judge did not disclose the consultation afterward and permit the parties to respond.

## 5. Contact with an Unrepresented Parent

> **From:** Allison Grant, Partner  
> **To:** You  
> **Subject:** Custody proposal delivered to unrepresented father
>
> Our client wanted to resolve a custody dispute amicably before the next court date. At her request, I met with the child's father, who did not have a lawyer. I presented our client's proposed arrangement and asked whether he would agree.
>
> He asked whether the proposal was fair. I told him that he should hire his own lawyer if he wanted advice about fairness, and I gave him no other advice. He hired counsel, and the matter later became contentious.
>
> Could I be disciplined for the meeting?

**Which response should the associate give?**

- **A.** Yes. A lawyer may not ask an unrepresented opposing party to agree to a client's proposal.
- **B.** Yes. A lawyer may not advise an unrepresented opposing party to obtain counsel.
- **C.** No. The only advice given to the unrepresented person was to obtain counsel.
- **D.** No. A lawyer may give any legal advice to an unrepresented opposing party.

## 6. Settlement Funds and the Client's Bank

> **From:** Rachel Kim, Partner  
> **To:** You  
> **Subject:** Bank demand against settlement proceeds
>
> I settled a client's personal-injury case and deposited the $10,000 settlement check into our client trust account. The next day, a bank wrote to say that the client was three months behind on a mortgage. It demanded $3,000 from the settlement proceeds to avoid foreclosure.
>
> We do not represent the client in the mortgage matter. The client did not dispute owing the bank $3,000, but he believed the mortgaged building was worthless and that the bank would not foreclose. He instructed me to deduct our fee and send him the balance. I followed those instructions.
>
> Was that proper?

**Which response should the associate give?**

- **A.** No. The client's acknowledgment of the debt required the lawyer to pay the bank from the settlement.
- **B.** No. The lawyer knew the client was effectively forcing the bank to foreclose.
- **C.** Yes, because the lawyer did not represent the client in the mortgage matter.
- **D.** Yes, because the bank had not established a right to the specific personal-injury proceeds.

## 7. Fraudulent Charges by a Former Partner

> **From:** Naomi Brooks, Managing Partner  
> **To:** You  
> **Subject:** Reporting obligation after billing fraud
>
> We learned that one of our partners charged personal expenses to a client and falsely described them as representation expenses. When confronted with the evidence, the partner resigned.
>
> We told the client and refunded every improper charge. The client does not object to reporting the former partner to disciplinary authorities but has left the decision to us.
>
> Are we required to report?

**Which response should the associate give?**

- **A.** Yes. Lawyers must report every violation of a professional-conduct rule.
- **B.** Yes. The conduct raises a substantial question about the former partner's honesty, trustworthiness, or fitness as a lawyer.
- **C.** No. Promptly refunding the charges eliminated any reporting obligation.
- **D.** No. The remaining partners owe the former partner a fiduciary duty not to report conduct that occurred during the partnership.

## 8. A Settlement that Disqualifies the Lawyer

> **From:** Rebecca Stone, Partner  
> **To:** You  
> **Subject:** Divorce settlement condition
>
> I represent a wife in a bitter divorce involving property and custody. After the first day of trial, opposing counsel offered a settlement that is better than any likely judgment. Our client wants to accept it.
>
> There is one condition: I must agree not to represent her in any later proceeding, brought by either party, to modify or enforce the divorce decree. I believe accepting the offer is in her best interest and am considering recommending it.
>
> May I do so?

**Which response should the associate give?**

- **A.** No, unless the client gives informed consent to the conflict created by the condition.
- **B.** No. The settlement improperly restricts the lawyer's right to represent the client in the future.
- **C.** Yes. The restriction is permissible because it applies only to later proceedings in the same matter.
- **D.** Yes. The lawyer's reasonable belief that settlement benefits the client makes the restriction permissible.

## 9. The Missed Appeal Deadline

> **From:** Thomas Reed, Partner  
> **To:** You  
> **Subject:** Proposed resolution of my missed deadline
>
> I agreed to handle a pro bono appeal from the denial of unemployment benefits, but I missed the filing deadline. After discovering the mistake, I offered the client the amount he would have received if the appeal had succeeded in exchange for releasing his potential malpractice claim.
>
> The proposed agreement was in writing. It advised him to seek independent counsel and gave him as much time as he needed to do so. He did not consult another lawyer, but he signed the following week. I then paid the agreed amount.
>
> Am I subject to discipline?

**Which response should the associate give?**

- **A.** Yes. A lawyer may never settle a potential malpractice claim with a client.
- **B.** Yes. The agreement is invalid because the client did not actually consult independent counsel.
- **C.** No. The lawyer advised the client in writing to seek independent counsel and gave him a reasonable opportunity to do so.
- **D.** No, solely because the lawyer fully compensated the client's expected loss.

## 10. Proof of Perjury in an Unrelated Case

> **From:** Sofia Bennett, Partner  
> **To:** You  
> **Subject:** Client gave us proof of an opponent's old perjury
>
> In our breach-of-contract case, the client gave me incontrovertible proof that the opposing party committed perjury in unrelated litigation several years ago. That earlier case ended in the opposing party's favor. Neither our client nor our firm participated in it.
>
> Our client and the opposing party once had a close personal and business relationship. I believe disclosure could hurt our client's interests because a court might draw negative inferences from that relationship. I have therefore kept the information confidential.
>
> Is that proper?

**Which response should the associate give?**

- **A.** Yes. The information relates to the current representation, and disclosure could be detrimental to the client's interests.
- **B.** Yes, solely because neither the client nor the lawyer participated in the earlier litigation.
- **C.** No. Knowledge that someone committed fraud on a tribunal always creates a duty to disclose it.
- **D.** No. The information must be disclosed because it is not protected by the attorney-client privilege.

## 11. The Million-Dollar Verdict Advertisement

> **From:** Jordan Ellis, Marketing Partner  
> **To:** You  
> **Subject:** Review our blog advertisement
>
> A plaintiffs' lawyer placed this accurate statement in a consumer blog advertisement: "In the last three years, I have obtained five jury verdicts awarding more than $1 million each in medical-malpractice cases."
>
> The advertisement also said: "The merits of claims and the amount of damages vary with each case. Past results do not guarantee any future recovery." After six months, the lawyer surveyed prospective clients, and none reported seeing the advertisement.
>
> Does the advertisement create a disciplinary problem?

**Which response should the associate give?**

- **A.** Yes. Mentioning past results necessarily creates unjustified expectations.
- **B.** No. Even without the disclaimer, no reasonable person could interpret past results as suggesting similar future outcomes.
- **C.** No. The accurate advertisement includes a disclaimer designed to avoid unjustified expectations.
- **D.** No, but only because no prospective client reported seeing the advertisement.

## 12. Testimony Before Congress

> **From:** Caroline Wu, General Counsel  
> **To:** You  
> **Subject:** Our outside lawyer's copper-industry testimony
>
> Our company operates in the uranium industry and retains outside counsel for all regulatory and litigation work. We are considering acquiring a copper mine, so we asked that lawyer to testify before a congressional committee that the copper industry is overregulated.
>
> The lawyer truthfully gave that testimony, registered under his own name, and did not disclose that he appeared for a client. He later billed us for the testimony.
>
> Was his conduct proper?

**Which response should the associate give?**

- **A.** Yes. Confidentiality prohibited the lawyer from identifying the client.
- **B.** Yes. Attorney-client privilege prohibited disclosure of the client's identity in this setting.
- **C.** No. By testifying, the lawyer waived the company's attorney-client privilege.
- **D.** No. The lawyer should have disclosed that he was appearing and testifying in a representative capacity.

## 13. The Internal Investigation and Ongoing Billing Fraud

> **From:** Henry Cole, Investigations Partner  
> **To:** You  
> **Subject:** Reporting out after board refuses to act
>
> A health-care corporation retained us after the state attorney general opened a Medicaid-fraud investigation. Our engagement covers investigating the allegations, advising the company, and defending any resulting civil or criminal litigation.
>
> An employee told us that the company president directed employees to inflate Medicaid bills. Our record review confirmed that the fraud is ongoing. I reported the issue to general counsel and then to the board. The board considered it and declined to act.
>
> I know the fraud will continue and is reasonably certain to substantially injure the corporation. Without obtaining the employee's informed consent, I disclosed the information to the attorney general.
>
> Am I subject to discipline?

**Which response should the associate give?**

- **A.** Yes, because the employee did not give informed consent to disclosure.
- **B.** Yes, because the firm was retained to investigate the alleged violations and to defend the corporation in proceedings arising from them.
- **C.** No. Reporting first to the board permitted disclosure to the government once the board refused to act.
- **D.** No. Information about conduct reasonably certain to substantially injure the corporation may always be disclosed.

## 14. The Drafting Lawyer as Probate Counsel and Witness

> **From:** Michael Torres, Estate-Planning Partner  
> **To:** You  
> **Subject:** Can I handle the probate if I must testify?
>
> I represented a client for years, drafted her will, and served as one of its two subscribing witnesses. The will left 10% to her housekeeper, 10% to her son and sole heir, and the residue to charity.
>
> After the client died, the executor asked me to handle the probate. The son plans to contest the will on the ground that his mother lacked capacity. I believe she was fully competent and expect to testify at trial. The other subscribing witness died before the client.
>
> May I represent the executor in the contested probate?

**Which response should the associate give?**

- **A.** No. The lawyer will be a necessary witness on a contested factual issue.
- **B.** No. Representing the executor is necessarily adverse to the client's heir.
- **C.** Yes. Representation is permitted because the lawyer is the only surviving witness to execution.
- **D.** Yes. Representation is permitted because the lawyer's testimony supports the will's validity.

## 15. Comments About a Judicial Candidate

> **From:** Grace Miller, Managing Partner  
> **To:** You  
> **Subject:** Newspaper quote about judicial candidate
>
> A reporter asked one of our lawyers for his general impressions of candidates in a nonpartisan judicial election. Without independently investigating their backgrounds, he made this statement about a prosecutor who graduated from law school two years ago:
>
> "It takes years of experience to develop the wisdom needed to serve as a judge. Because this prosecutor has practiced for only two years, I do not believe he is ready to become a judge."
>
> The lawyer honestly holds that opinion. Could he be disciplined for saying it?

**Which response should the associate give?**

- **A.** No. A lawyer may give an honest and candid opinion about a judicial candidate's professional fitness.
- **B.** No. The First Amendment completely immunizes lawyers from discipline for statements about judges and judicial candidates.
- **C.** Yes. A lawyer may not publicly criticize a candidate's qualifications for judicial office.
- **D.** Yes. The lawyer first had to conduct a formal investigation into the candidate's qualifications.
