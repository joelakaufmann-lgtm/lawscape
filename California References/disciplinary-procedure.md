# California State Bar Disciplinary Procedure — Reference Pointer

**GAP NOTICE.** The training corpus delivered to this skill did not include the full text of the California State Bar Court Rules of Procedure or the State Bar's discipline-system procedural rules. This file is a **reference pointer** to the canonical primary sources. Do **not** invent or paraphrase procedural text. If a question requires quoting operative procedural language, ask the user to supply the rule, pull it from an official or reliable legal source, or disclose the gap.

## Primary Sources

### State Bar Court Rules of Procedure

- **Rules of Procedure of the State Bar of California** (State Bar Court Rules), as currently in force. These govern original disciplinary, reinstatement, moral character, and related proceedings before the State Bar Court (Hearing Department and Review Department).
- Divisions typically include (subject to change):
  - **Rules 2000s** — General Provisions and Commencement of Proceedings.
  - **Rules 5000s–5300s** — Disciplinary proceedings (notice of disciplinary charges, answer, discovery, pretrial, trial, decision, review).
  - **Rules for Reinstatement** — Petitions for reinstatement after disbarment or resignation with disciplinary charges pending.
  - **Rules for Moral Character Proceedings**.
  - **Attorney Diversion and Assistance Program (Lawyer Assistance Program, LAP)** rules — alternative discipline and treatment-based resolutions.

### Investigation and Office of Chief Trial Counsel (OCTC)

- Complaints are received and investigated by the **Office of Chief Trial Counsel (OCTC)**, an arm of the State Bar.
- **Business and Professions Code § 6044** (investigative authority of the State Bar).
- **Business and Professions Code § 6068(i)** (attorney's duty to cooperate with State Bar investigations).
- **Business and Professions Code § 6086.1** (confidentiality of State Bar investigations until formal charges).

### Supreme Court Review

- State Bar Court decisions are subject to review by the **California Supreme Court**. Review is governed by **Cal. R. Ct. 9.13 and 9.14** and the State Bar Rules of Procedure.
- The Supreme Court has the ultimate authority to impose discipline (suspension, disbarment) under **Business and Professions Code § 6100 et seq.**

### Standing Committee on Professional Responsibility and Conduct (COPRAC)

- **COPRAC** issues advisory Formal Ethics Opinions. Its rules of procedure govern how opinions are drafted, circulated for public comment, approved, and published.
- COPRAC opinions are **advisory only** — they are not binding on the State Bar Court, the Supreme Court, or practitioners in disciplinary proceedings. They are persuasive and frequently cited.
- For procedure to request a COPRAC opinion or hypothetical guidance, see the State Bar's website (State Bar of California, Ethics).

### Local Bar Association Ethics Opinions

- The **Los Angeles County Bar Association (LACBA)** and the **Bar Association of San Francisco (BASF)** (through the Legal Ethics Committee of the Bar Association of San Francisco) also issue ethics opinions. These are local advisory opinions, persuasive only, and do not bind the State Bar. They are commonly cited in California ethics literature alongside COPRAC opinions.
- **San Diego County Bar Association** and other county bar associations also issue ethics opinions.
- This skill does not contain the full text of local bar ethics opinions; when they are directly on point, verify them from reliable sources before relying on them.

## Typical Procedural Timeline

1. **Complaint filed** with OCTC (intake, initial review).
2. **Investigation** by OCTC — attorney receives a letter requesting response; failure to cooperate is itself a § 6068(i) violation.
3. **Closure, Admonition, or Notice of Disciplinary Charges (NDC).** If OCTC proceeds formally, an NDC is filed with the State Bar Court.
4. **Answer and pretrial** in the Hearing Department.
5. **Trial** before a Hearing Department judge.
6. **Hearing Department Decision** — recommended discipline.
7. **Review Department Appeal** — optional.
8. **California Supreme Court Review** — discretionary.
9. **Imposition of Discipline** by the Supreme Court (disbarment, suspension) or State Bar (reproval).

## Citation Format

- State Bar Court Rules of Procedure: `State Bar R. Proc. [Rule Number]`.
- Business and Professions Code: `Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § [number]`.
- State Bar Court decisions (published): `In the Matter of [Name], [volume] Cal. State Bar Ct. Rptr. [page] (Rev. Dep't [year])`.
- California Supreme Court discipline opinions: Standard Bluebook form.

## When to Consult This Area

Load or reference this file when:

- A lawyer has been the subject of a complaint or notice of investigation by OCTC.
- A lawyer is considering a self-report under Bus. & Prof. Code § 6068(o) or CRPC 8.3.
- The question involves State Bar Court procedural rules, the Review Department, or Supreme Court discipline review.
- The question involves a reinstatement petition after disbarment or resignation.
- The question involves a COPRAC opinion request, local bar ethics opinion, or moral character proceeding.
