# Canada test pack — five email assignments

Version: canada-0.1.0-review. Approximately 100–200 words each. Text is retained for self-comparison and human review, not automatically assessed.

## CA-EMAIL-ON · Who is handling tomorrow’s LTB hearing?

**Ontario · To: Supervising lawyer · Needs practitioner review**

Write a 100–200 word internal email recommending the hearing assignment, identifying Riley’s permissible support role and the notification still needed. Distinguish the confirmed facts from anything you would check.

### Fictional record

- **ON-E1:** A tenant’s Toronto LTB hearing is tomorrow. Priya is an Ontario paralegal in good standing; this file is within her authorized scope, and her competence, preparation and availability are confirmed.
- **ON-E2:** The client instructs Priya to represent them. Riley is a paid unlicensed office assistant; no representation exemption applies. The client wants Riley to help organize papers and take notes without making submissions.
- **ON-E3:** The application was filed before Priya was retained and named no representative. The file contains no written notice to the LTB or the other parties of the new representation.

### Practitioner rubric

Human review only: 0 = absent or materially wrong; 1 = partly sound but incomplete; 2 = accurate, supported and practical. 10 points is a discussion aid, not a credential or automated pass.

- Identifies Priya as the instructed, eligible representative on the supplied facts. (2 points)
- Keeps Riley’s support role distinct from making submissions or borrowing a licence. (2 points)
- Identifies written notification to the LTB and other parties as outstanding. (2 points)
- Uses the record accurately without inventing a merits outcome or automatic adjournment. (2 points)
- Provides a clear, courteous and feasible next-step email within roughly 100–200 words. (2 points)

Critical issues for discussion:

- Assigning Riley to represent despite the stipulated lack of exemption.
- Saying only lawyers may appear at the LTB.

### Sample response — not the only acceptable wording

Subject: Tomorrow’s LTB hearing — assignment and notice

I recommend that Priya represent the tenant. The file confirms her current licence, authorized scope, preparation and availability, and the client has instructed her to act. We should record that assignment and confirm tomorrow’s hearing arrangements with her and the client.

Riley can help organize the hearing papers and take notes in the agreed support role. Riley should not make submissions for the tenant: the record expressly states that no representation exemption applies. Priya’s licence cannot be used as Riley’s authorization.

The application did not identify Priya, and I cannot find notice of her appointment. I will arrange written notification to the LTB and the other parties, then retain the notification record and check for any hearing directions. If an availability problem arises, I will raise it promptly so we can address representation lawfully rather than assume an adjournment.

Please confirm that this is the staffing plan you want implemented.

### Authored partner guidance

The strongest reply assigns the work using the actual licence and client instructions, then identifies the unfinished notification. It does not confuse Riley’s usefulness with permission to advocate. Your prose has not been automatically graded; compare it with the rubric and discuss any defensible alternative.

Acceptable alternatives:

- A concise email requesting the supervisor to arrange notice rather than offering to send it yourself is acceptable.
- Further logistical checks are sensible if identified as checks, not missing established licence or competence facts.

- **Who may represent an LTB party?:** Licensed Ontario lawyers and paralegals in good standing, and qualifying exempt representatives, may represent a party. A suspended licence does not permit representation. Eligibility does not establish competence, client instructions, availability, or success. Specific exemptions and any applicable statutory priority require review.
  - [Tribunals Ontario — Representation before the LTB](https://tribunalsontario.ca/documents/ltb/Practice%20Directions/Practice%20Direction%20on%20Representation%20before%20the%20LTB.html), Representatives; Licensed Representative; Unlicensed Representatives
- **Support is not representation:** A support person may assist a participant without making submissions on their behalf. New representatives not identified in the application must notify the LTB and other parties in writing. These questions distinguish the stipulated roles; they do not decide every licensing exemption or witness issue.
  - [Tribunals Ontario — Representation before the LTB](https://tribunalsontario.ca/documents/ltb/Practice%20Directions/Practice%20Direction%20on%20Representation%20before%20the%20LTB.html), Support Persons; Representative’s Responsibilities

## CA-EMAIL-REF · Explain the referral without selling the fee

**British Columbia · To: Client, Morgan · Needs practitioner review**

Write a 100–200 word client email explaining the proposed referral and payment, the effect on the client’s bill, and the decision you need. Do not treat consent as already obtained.

### Fictional record

- **REF-E1:** Morgan’s Vancouver business needs a specialist for a licensing dispute. The referring lawyer lacks the specialist expertise; no conflict caused the referral. The proposed receiving lawyer is another BC lawyer in a separate firm.
- **REF-E2:** The receiving lawyer would charge Morgan $3,000 for the same defined work with or without a referral fee. A proposed $300 inter-lawyer payment would come from that $3,000, not be added to it. Reasonableness is stipulated for this exercise.
- **REF-E3:** Morgan has not yet been informed or consented. The specialist is also willing to accept a no-referral-fee arrangement. No transfer of the client file or payment has occurred.

### Practitioner rubric

Human review only: 0 = absent or materially wrong; 1 = partly sound but incomplete; 2 = accurate, supported and practical. 10 points is a discussion aid, not a credential or automated pass.

- Explains the expertise-based reason and avoids inventing a conflict. (2 points)
- Clearly separates the $300 inter-lawyer payment from the unchanged $3,000 client fee. (2 points)
- Seeks informed consent instead of presuming or pressuring it. (2 points)
- Offers the stated no-fee alternative and seeks instructions before the handoff. (2 points)
- Uses clear client language without claiming a regulatory endorsement or guaranteed outcome. (2 points)

Critical issues for discussion:

- Adding $300 to the client’s total.
- Treating the client’s silence as consent.

### Sample response — not the only acceptable wording

Subject: Proposed specialist referral and your instructions

Your licensing dispute would benefit from a lawyer with the specialist experience described in our discussion. I propose referring you to the identified BC specialist for that reason; this referral is not being made because of a conflict.

The specialist has proposed a $3,000 fee for the defined work. That total would be the same whether or not a referral fee is paid. If you agree, the specialist would pay our firm $300 from that amount for the referral. It would not be an additional $300 charge to you.

We have not treated your consent as given. Please let me know whether you understand and agree to this arrangement, or whether you would prefer the available option with no referral payment. You may also ask questions or discuss another suitable lawyer.

Once we have your instructions, we can confirm the engagement and arrange an authorized handoff. No referral payment or file transfer has occurred.

### Authored partner guidance

A useful client email makes the money understandable and leaves the decision open. Selecting a specialist and agreeing to an inter-lawyer fee are separate choices. Compare your text with the proposed response; this is authored guidance, not an assessment of your actual prose.

Acceptable alternatives:

- It is acceptable to recommend the no-fee option while accurately explaining the available arrangement.
- A request to discuss the arrangement before deciding is also a valid next step.

- **Referral fee conditions:** An expertise-and-ability referral not caused by conflict may attract a reasonable fee if the client is informed and consents and the total client fee does not increase. BC rule only. Each exercise assumes separate BC law firms and no other fee-sharing arrangement. Consent alone does not establish every condition.
  - [Law Society of British Columbia — BC Code, Chapter 3](https://www.lawsociety.bc.ca/for-lawyers/act-rules-and-code/code-of-professional-conduct/chapter-3-%E2%80%93-relationship-to-clients/), Rule 3.6-6(a)–(b)

## CA-EMAIL-REC · A meeting without audio

**British Columbia · To: Client, Avery · Needs practitioner review**

Write a 100–200 word pre-meeting email responding to Avery’s preference. Explain how the meeting will proceed and how a later change would be handled, without claiming that notice alone clears every legal issue.

### Fictional record

- **REC-E1:** The Victoria lawyer proposed recording tomorrow’s advice call, but recording has not started. Avery replies: “I would prefer written notes. Will I still be able to have the meeting?”
- **REC-E2:** The office can conduct the meeting with manual notes; no recording is required. Another lawyer may join later. Recording and transcription functions can remain off throughout.
- **REC-E3:** No vendor assessment or separate privacy analysis has been completed. No confidential information has been sent to an outside transcription service.

### Practitioner rubric

Human review only: 0 = absent or materially wrong; 1 = partly sound but incomplete; 2 = accurate, supported and practical. 10 points is a discussion aid, not a credential or automated pass.

- Confirms that the meeting can proceed with manual notes and recording off. (2 points)
- Acknowledges the preference courteously without treating it as misconduct. (2 points)
- Explains that any later proposal to record requires prior information to relevant participants. (2 points)
- Preserves the separate confidentiality/information-handling review boundary. (2 points)
- Gives practical next steps without inventing recording, a data transfer, or a universal consent rule. (2 points)

Critical issues for discussion:

- Suggesting secret capture or automatic recording on joining.
- Saying the recording-notice rule approves all transcription services.

### Sample response — not the only acceptable wording

Subject: Tomorrow’s meeting — we can use written notes

Yes, the meeting can go ahead without recording. We will keep the recording and transcription functions off and take manual notes instead. Thank you for letting us know your preference; audio is not required for this meeting.

I will confirm that arrangement with anyone assisting us. If another lawyer joins, we will explain how the meeting is being documented. If a recording is proposed later, we will raise it before any capture begins and discuss the proposal with the relevant participants rather than switch it on unexpectedly.

Any proposed use of an outside transcription service would also need separate consideration of confidentiality and information handling. Telling participants about recording would not, by itself, settle those questions. No information has been sent to such a service.

Please attend at the scheduled time. Let me know if there is anything else about the meeting arrangements you would like us to address.

### Authored partner guidance

This task rewards a workable response to the client’s preference. Keep the notice rule distinct from a broader privacy or consent analysis, and do not invent a need for audio. Your email is retained for self-comparison and practitioner discussion; the application does not grade its wording.

Acceptable alternatives:

- A shorter email with an offer to discuss documentation needs is acceptable if the recording-off arrangement stays clear.
- Do not require the client to resolve internal vendor compliance questions.

- **Inform before recording:** Before using a device to record a conversation with a client or another lawyer, the BC lawyer must first inform that person. Other information-handling obligations remain separate. This is a prior-notification rule, not a blanket consent or privacy-law opinion. No actual recording takes place. Manual notes are a supplied exercise option.
  - [Law Society of British Columbia — BC Code, Chapter 7](https://www.lawsociety.bc.ca/for-lawyers/act-rules-and-code/code-of-professional-conduct/chapter-7-%E2%80%93-relationship-to-the-society-and-other-lawyers/), Rule 7.2-3

## CA-EMAIL-SK · Confirm the French-language instructions

**Saskatchewan · To: Client, Camille · Needs practitioner review**

Write a 100–200 word client email confirming the instruction, the competence issue and the procedural step still required. Make the deadline understandable and avoid promising an order already exists.

### Fictional record

- **SK-E1:** Camille is accused in a Saskatchewan criminal proceeding. For this exercise French is Camille’s language under section 530(1). Camille requests French-language representation and trial, although conversational English is comfortable.
- **SK-E2:** The first appearance has occurred. The appearance at which the trial date will be set is five days away. No section 530 application or order is in the file.
- **SK-E3:** The current lawyer is not competent to conduct the required representation in French. A competent French-speaking Saskatchewan criminal lawyer is available to discuss accepting the retainer; Camille has not yet approved the proposed handoff.

### Practitioner rubric

Human review only: 0 = absent or materially wrong; 1 = partly sound but incomplete; 2 = accurate, supported and practical. 10 points is a discussion aid, not a credential or automated pass.

- Accurately confirms the client’s French-language choice without overriding it because of English fluency. (2 points)
- Candidly identifies the current lawyer’s competence limitation and proposes competent assistance. (2 points)
- Seeks the client’s instructions for the proposed handoff rather than announcing it as completed. (2 points)
- Identifies the trial-date-setting appearance as the relevant subsection (1) deadline and plans prompt action. (2 points)
- Distinguishes a retainer, application and court order; communicates clearly without a guaranteed result. (2 points)

Critical issues for discussion:

- Switching to English without the client’s instruction.
- Calling the prospective lawyer’s availability an issued court order.
- Treating the first appearance as an absolute bar to a timely application here.

### Sample response — not the only acceptable wording

Subject: Your French-language representation and trial request

I confirm that you wish to receive the required legal services and have your criminal trial in French. Your ability to converse in English does not change that instruction. I am not able to conduct this representation competently in French, so I propose discussing the retainer with the available French-speaking Saskatchewan criminal lawyer, with your approval.

There is also a separate court step. On the supplied facts, the section 530(1) application should be made no later than the appearance at which your trial date is set. That appearance is five days away. We need to arrange responsibility for the application promptly; retaining a French-speaking lawyer will not itself produce an order.

Please confirm whether you approve the proposed handoff. We will coordinate the immediate steps, verify the application’s filing and court status, and confirm the arrangements with you. At present, the file contains neither the application nor an order.

### Authored partner guidance

The strongest reply keeps three things visible: the client’s choice, competent services, and a procedural step with its own timing. Do not treat one as proof that the others are complete. A practitioner should assess the local procedure and the proposed transition before approving this assignment.

Acceptable alternatives:

- A request for an urgent client call can be appropriate if the email still explains the upcoming checkpoint.
- A competent team arrangement may be considered if the practitioner confirms it actually meets the required service and retainer obligations.

- **Client language choice and competence:** Advise appropriately about language rights. A lawyer must be competent to provide the required services in the official language selected by the client. The client chooses. The exercises concern criminal proceedings and lawyers; other forums, languages and practitioner categories need separate analysis.
  - [Law Society of Saskatchewan — Code of Professional Conduct for Lawyers](https://www.lawsociety.sk.ca/wp-content/uploads/CodeOfProfessionalConduct.pdf#page=26), Rules 3.2-2A/B; commentary [1]–[3], printed p. 26
- **Language application and order:** For the stated French-language accused, a timely section 530(1) application engages the mandatory order framework. The stated deadline is the appearance when the trial date is set; a late request requires separate section 530(4) consideration. First-appearance advice and the application deadline are distinct. A competent lawyer is not itself a court order. Late relief is not guaranteed.
  - [Justice Canada — Criminal Code, section 530](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-46/section-530.html), Section 530(1), (3), (4)

## CA-EMAIL-UND · The client wants the funds today

**British Columbia · To: Supervising BC lawyer · Needs practitioner review**

Write a 100–200 word internal email explaining whether release can occur now and the steps you recommend. Address the proposed variation without assuming it has been accepted. Identify what would change if a breach were discovered.

### Fictional record

- **UND-E1:** The Nanaimo office accepted a written trust condition: “Hold the $12,000 until you receive my written confirmation that the specified release event has occurred.” This quoted sentence is a fictional exercise term, not a quotation from a conduct rule.
- **UND-E2:** The required confirmation has not arrived. The client asks for immediate payment. The lawyer who imposed the condition has not answered the office’s request to vary it. The existing condition can still be honoured.
- **UND-E3:** The funds remain held and no breach has occurred on the supplied facts. The record does not establish a present right to return the funds or cancel the commitment unilaterally.

### Practitioner rubric

Human review only: 0 = absent or materially wrong; 1 = partly sound but incomplete; 2 = accurate, supported and practical. 10 points is a discussion aid, not a credential or automated pass.

- Identifies that the confirmation required by the exact condition is missing. (2 points)
- Does not treat the client’s demand or unanswered request as effective variation. (2 points)
- Proposes continued compliance and an appropriately agreed, documented variation if pursued. (2 points)
- Keeps the no-breach fact distinct from the conditional discussion of remediation/reporting. (2 points)
- Qualifies any reporting discussion for confidentiality/privilege and avoids a universal return-funds escape. (2 points)

Critical issues for discussion:

- Releasing the $12,000 before the stated condition is satisfied or effectively varied.
- Saying a variation request equals consent.
- Recommending unrestricted disclosure of the client file if an issue arises.

### Sample response — not the only acceptable wording

Subject: $12,000 trust condition — release is not yet authorized

The required written confirmation has not arrived, so the condition we accepted does not presently permit release. The client’s request does not change that condition. Our unanswered variation request is also not an agreement.

I recommend that we keep the funds held under the existing terms, explain the outstanding step to the client, and follow up with the lawyer who imposed the condition. If an appropriate variation is agreed, we should confirm its precise terms in writing before acting on it. This record does not establish a separate right to return the funds or cancel the commitment ourselves.

No breach has occurred on the supplied facts. If we discover one, we should preserve an accurate record, address remediation and assess the reporting duty for an unconsented, unwaived breach, including the confidentiality and privilege qualification. We should not assume that later performance alone erases the issue or permits unrestricted disclosure.

Please confirm who will handle the follow-up.

### Authored partner guidance

The exact condition controls this file. A sound response distinguishes a requested change from an agreed one and keeps hypothetical breach reporting separate from the actual no-breach facts. Use the rubric with your practitioner; no text-matching score has been applied.

Acceptable alternatives:

- A carefully framed request for clarification or a proposed alternative condition is acceptable without unilateral release.
- Any proposed property-return route must be supported by the particular terms and reviewed; it is not the default answer here.

- **Commitments must be kept:** Do not promise what cannot be fulfilled. Honour undertakings and accepted trust conditions; client pressure or an unanswered variation request does not release the obligation. Writing is recommended in the commentary; these facts expressly stipulate an undertaking. Any property-return route must fit the actual trust condition, not a universal cancellation rule.
  - [Law Society of British Columbia — BC Code, Chapter 7](https://www.lawsociety.bc.ca/for-lawyers/act-rules-and-code/code-of-professional-conduct/chapter-7-%E2%80%93-relationship-to-the-society-and-other-lawyers/), Rule 7.2-11; commentary [1], [3]–[5]
- **Breach and reporting:** For an unconsented, unwaived breach of an undertaking or trust condition, assess the reporting duty, subject to solicitor-client confidentiality or privilege. Remediation does not itself erase the event. Disclosure scope and any exception require careful practitioner review.
  - [Law Society of British Columbia — BC Code, Chapter 7](https://www.lawsociety.bc.ca/for-lawyers/act-rules-and-code/code-of-professional-conduct/chapter-7-%E2%80%93-relationship-to-the-society-and-other-lawyers/), Rule 7.1-3 opening words and paragraph (a.1)
