# Feedback: Canadian Practice Files for LawScape

**September 27, 2026**  
**Proposal reviewed:** *LawScape: Canadian Practice Files — Implementation Plan*, v2, September 26, 2026.  
**Status:** Product and curriculum feedback; proposed content has not yet been authored or legally validated.

## Executive summary

This is a good start. The proposal gives Canada a meaningful place in LawScape by connecting professional-responsibility decisions to ongoing client matters. Its emphasis on province-specific rules, consequential choices, and useful debriefs provides a strong foundation.

I would build on that foundation with **20–30 multiple-choice ethics questions** and **5–10 short, email-style writing assignments**. Together, these would let players identify an issue, explain what they would do, and then apply that judgment as a matter develops. The Canadian experience should also have a distinctive sense of place—through its offices, people, language, and regional settings—that goes well beyond maple syrup or a few familiar symbols. **Canada should be an explicit roadmap item**, beginning with the Ontario, British Columbia, and Saskatchewan episodes proposed here.

## 1. Add a focused ethics question bank

Target **25 questions initially**, with a working range of **20–30**. The proposal's five topics provide a practical starting structure:

| Proposed topic | Initial question target |
| --- | ---: |
| Ontario: staffing, professional roles, and appropriate assignments | 5 |
| British Columbia: referrals and fee arrangements | 5 |
| British Columbia: recording and participant notice | 5 |
| Saskatchewan: language rights and implementing client instructions | 5 |
| British Columbia: undertakings and trust conditions | 5 |

Use short workplace scenarios with plausible answer choices. Each question should explain the decisive fact, why the preferred answer works, and why the alternatives fall short. Include situations where the next step is to ask for more information. Every question needs a clear jurisdiction label and reviewed authority; the pack should not imply that one answer applies throughout Canada.

These questions should complement the continuing matters, providing quick practice before players encounter the same issue in a more involved file.

## 2. Add short email-style writing assignments

Create **5–10 assignments**, beginning with one for each proposed module and adding follow-up emails as the files develop. Aim for approximately **100–200 words per response** so the exercise feels like everyday professional correspondence.

The first five could ask the player to:

1. Email a supervising lawyer with a proposed staffing assignment and the checks still needed.
2. Explain a proposed referral and any fee arrangement to a client in plain language.
3. Send a meeting email addressing the proposed recording and a participant's concern.
4. Confirm a client's language instructions and explain the next steps in the file.
5. Respond to a request that affects an existing undertaking or trust condition.

Additional assignments could introduce changed availability, a client's objection, or a request to vary a commitment. Feedback should address issue recognition, the relevant facts, a practical next step, clarity, and professional tone. Include a reviewed sample response and acceptable alternatives. Use the established draft → commit → partner reply format; authored feedback and self-comparison can support the first release without making it dependent on live AI grading.

## 3. Give the Canadian experience depth and personality

The theme should emerge from the practice environment as well as the curriculum. Develop regionally grounded offices, neighbourhoods, weather, architecture, and travel details—for example, a coastal BC setting, an Ontario urban practice, and a prairie office in Saskatchewan. Give clients and colleagues varied backgrounds, distinct personalities, and credible local concerns. Keep LawScape's office humour and partner pressure.

Use Canadian spelling and locally appropriate professional terminology. Make English and French meaningful where the episode calls for them, while distinguishing interface language from the legal issue being taught. Seek local contributor feedback on the visual and cultural details. Any Indigenous-specific setting or storyline should be developed with appropriate contributors and review.

Familiar Canadian references can appear as occasional touches, but the overall identity should come from a believable place to work and learn.

## 4. Add Canada to the roadmap

Add **Canada — professional responsibility and practice skills** as a planned jurisdiction initiative, with four milestones:

1. **Scope and review:** confirm the three initial provincial settings, contributors, reviewers, and source versions.
2. **Starter curriculum:** author and review 20–30 ethics questions and 5–10 email assignments.
3. **Canadian practice experience:** connect those exercises to the proposed continuing matters and introduce the regional visual and narrative direction.
4. **Pilot and expansion:** test with Canadian practitioners and learners, revise the content, and consider further provinces and territories with their own review.

Track question-bank readiness, writing-assignment readiness, and continuing-matter readiness separately. Canada should remain marked **planned** until the relevant content has been reviewed, implemented, and tested; the initial three-province pilot is not comprehensive Canadian coverage.
